# Patterns — MrBeast Production

## Bottleneck Declaration
**When to use**: you need input/deliverable from another person or team before you can start.
**How**: tell them face-to-face they are the bottleneck and why (context → impact → deadline); agree a concrete date; then check in daily, asking for video evidence, until it's done.
**Trade-offs**: costs relationship friction if heavy-handed; the daily cadence prevents silent drift (the leading cause of failed videos).

## Critical Component Protection
**When to use**: a component is labeled essential to the video — whichever dept, however it got the label.
**How**: protect it at all costs; check in ~10x/day; build a backup in parallel; for physical goods avoid standard shipping (pay a courier); escalate problems to leadership the second they appear; never let it be a coin flip.
**Trade-offs**: fast risk reduction vs. time spent on the backup; correct because one missing island kills a million+ dollar video.

## Contractor Daily Tracking
**When to use**: any external/contractor build (stands, tanks, props) heading to filming day.
**How**: set a completion date with margin (never the shoot day), decide if the part is critical, build a backup if so, then ask for videos daily (twice a day if it's critical) to catch problems weeks early — "know weeks in advance if you're fucked, not days."
**Trade-offs**: higher touch per vendor, but you own the outcome either way — dump-and-forget puts the blame on you.

## Up-and-Over Communication
**When to use**: making a request across departments (e.g. production → writer).
**How**: route the ask through the department head first (creative → Tyler; production → Will), letting them know the option exists, then down. If you must go below first, call the head to inform them immediately.
**Trade-offs**: one extra hop per request vs. preventing double/triple work and confused assumptions (the 3-days-of-lava story).

## Decision Menu (Boss Ready)
**When to use**: needing a yes/no or choice from Jimmy or any senior decision-maker.
**How**: bring context + options, never a bare question. State the video, minute mark, budget confirmed with PM, one primary recommendation + 4 backups + alternate flavor (less-cool), with photo/mileage/data attached; end with one pointed question.
**Trade-offs**: prep effort up-front vs. dramatically higher chance of a fast, correct decision; the alternative ("what do you think of this Lexus") signals you don't care about the video's success.

## Pushing Through No
**When to use**: an external partner (Dollar Tree manager, venue owner) says "no" to what you need.
**How**: escalate within the org (fan employees, their kids, bosses up the chain), have Jimmy dm their social team, then try other locations/branches; stop only when all conceivable options are exhausted.
**Trade-offs**: persistent negotiation can read as pushiness; combined with the other tools it materially lifts the probability of a shoot happening.

## Escalating Form of Communication
**When to use**: matching the message's complexity to the channel.
**How**: trivial (bathroom TP) → text. Critical component one week out → minimum a multi-way call with video heads, ideally in person; call first, text if unanswered. Written doesn't count until they confirm they read it.
**Trade-offs**: heavier scheduling cost vs. eliminating miscommunication where it counts most.

## Stair Stepping Format
**When to use**: a progress/escalation video where stakes should rise.
**How**: open by showing the goal and lighting a $1 version → escalate $10/$50/$375/$1k/$10k → content beat → $40k/$100k → the world record payoff. Deviations allowed as long as each step progressively gets cooler and the payoff lands at the end.
**Trade-offs**: predictable arc (fine — viewers WANT it) but must rotate with other formats to avoid fatigue.

## Payoff Format (Last-To-Leave / Chase)
**When to use**: engineered retention by withholding the result.
**How**: set an unresolved outcome ("who leaves the circle last?"/"will they catch me?"), resolve only at the end; keep the middle un-boring. Works with money-at-stake (challenges) or pursuit (bounty hunter/military/FBI).
**Trade-offs**: massive retention upside; format dies if repeated back-to-back — space same-format videos apart.

## 50/50 Re-write Rule
**When to use**: a strong idea you estimate has only a coin-flip chance of working.
**How**: write another piece of content instead; content is unlimited, laziness is not an excuse.
**Trade-offs**: discards possibly-fine material; correct because the audience/srcppy upload barely moves the needle.
