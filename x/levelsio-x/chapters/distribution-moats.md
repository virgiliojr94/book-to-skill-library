# Distribution > moats in AI era

## Core Idea

As AI commoditizes software implementation, defensible value shifts to distribution (audience, attention, proprietary data). The classic software moat is eroding.

## In his words

> "With AI rapidly decimating moats, AGI on the horizon, monetizing distribution might become more important (AI itself says this), so it's interesting to see where this goes."
> — [2026-08-01](https://x.com/levelsio/status/2083548676593439014)

> "Barrier to entry for most bootstrapped indie businesses has been wiped out, anyone non-technical can build the same now for a $9/mo subscription, only thing they have remaining is distribution if they have an audience or distribution if they have specific skills to get users free or cheaply."
> — [2026-07-26](https://x.com/levelsio/status/2081441046844657787) (quote of @PatrickHaede, endorsing)

> "$7,032 + $6,474 X ad rev share (28d) $1,350 X subs revenue (28d) $4,406 book revenue $957 merch revenue... Highest X payout since January this year and steadily growing... (including book + merch now as they're directly linked to X and the majority source of their revenue now)"
> — [2026-08-01](https://x.com/levelsio/status/2083548676593439014)

## What it says

Software barriers that took years to build can now be replicated in weeks. What cannot be replicated: the audience, the brand, the distribution channel. X revenue (ads, subscriptions, book, merch) is presented as evidence that distribution itself generates income independent of the software products.

The framework implies a strategic pivot: invest in audience-building and proprietary data pipelines, not in features that can be cloned.

## Anti-patterns

- Interpreting "distribution matters more" as "product doesn't matter." He ships complex products (Photo AI, Hotelist) — distribution is the moat, but the product is the mechanism.
- Confusing audience size with distribution. He includes specific skills for getting users cheaply (TikTok, SEO) as alternatives to raw audience.
