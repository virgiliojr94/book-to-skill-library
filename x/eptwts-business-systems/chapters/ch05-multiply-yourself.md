# Chapter 5: Multiply Yourself

## Core Idea

Once direct work has produced real expertise, turn the method into a repeatable playbook that other capable people can execute. Align compensation with their success so expertise scales beyond the original operator's labor. ([Mar 22](https://x.com/eptwts/status/2035707973259944013), [Jun 20](https://x.com/eptwts/status/2068279446713348479))

## Frameworks Introduced

### “Multiply yourself”

Use this when demand exists, your own delivery is competent and repeatable, and personal labor has become the constraint.

1. Prove the work by doing it yourself.
2. Identify the decisions and actions that cause the result.
3. Encode them into a playbook with clear inputs, standards, and feedback.
4. Select an operator who already has complementary talent or access.
5. Enable rather than micromanage them.
6. Tie compensation to successful outcomes through a percentage or revenue share where appropriate.
7. Monitor whether the new operator's activity harms the original business through competition or quality drift.

The author first frames this as helping others do what you do for a cut of the profits, then describes it as streams that rely on expertise instead of labor. ([Mar 22](https://x.com/eptwts/status/2035707973259944013), [Jun 20a](https://x.com/eptwts/status/2068279446713348479), [Jun 20b](https://x.com/eptwts/status/2068279861077025061))

### Growth-operator playbook

The August example makes the abstraction concrete: find people with strong delivery skill, create distribution channels for them, provide a growth playbook that does not require constant supervision, help shape the offer, and take revenue share. ([Aug 21](https://x.com/eptwts/status/2090916538257740013))

## Mental Models

- **Expertise before leverage**: systems multiply what is already known; they do not replace competence.
- **Enablement over substitution**: help another capable person produce the result instead of cloning every task yourself.
- **Aligned upside**: outcome-linked compensation can make both parties better off when attribution and terms are clear.
- **Playbook as product**: the repeatable method is an asset even when it is not sold directly.

## Anti-patterns

- **Scaling before proof**: documenting a process that has not reliably worked.
- **Revenue share without control or attribution**: taking outcome risk when neither party can measure contribution.
- **Babysitting disguised as leverage**: a playbook that requires constant intervention has not reduced dependence on the expert. ([Aug 21](https://x.com/eptwts/status/2090916538257740013))
- **Creating self-competition**: the author explicitly notes that new operators can cut into the original profits. ([Jun 20a](https://x.com/eptwts/status/2068279446713348479))

## Evolution and Boundary

The March and June posts describe the general mechanism as mentorship plus a percentage. The August example adds selection, distribution infrastructure, offer creation, and “set and forget” operations. This is an evolution toward a concrete system, not evidence that every expertise business should use revenue share.

## Key Takeaways

1. Earn expertise through direct execution first.
2. Capture causal decisions, not merely a task checklist.
3. Pair the playbook with a capable operator.
4. Make incentives measurable and mutually beneficial.
5. Reject arrangements that only relocate supervision work.
6. Watch for cannibalization and quality drift.

## Connects To

- **Ch 1**: useful expertise is the prerequisite.
- **Ch 2**: distribution can be the repeatable capability supplied by the operator.
- **Ch 4**: the playbook and context layer preserve knowledge across operators and agents.
