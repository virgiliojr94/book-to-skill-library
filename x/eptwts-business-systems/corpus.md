[context: quoting @Engineering — their words, see raw.jsonl]
i analyzed the X algorithm, here are my findings...

the algo uses AI to predict 19 different actions users might take on a post

each action has a "weight", some matter way more than others

it multiplies prediction x weight & adds them up, this is your post's score

here's a break down each one:

---

1/ P(retweet) - "will they retweet this?"

essentially the AI deeming your tweet "retweetable"

---

2/ P(quote) - "will they quote tweet this?"

posting takes people want to add to. data they want to contextualize. takes they want to argue with or co-sign.

---

3/ P(follow_author) - "will they follow you after seeing this?"

it's triggered by demonstrating expertise & adding value. "if this is their random tweet, imagine what else they post."

---

4/ P(dwell_time) - "how long will they actually read this?"

the algo measures seconds spent on your post.

you can amplify this by posting threads, articles, lists, etc. anything that makes people stop scrolling and actually consume.

---

5/ P(video_view) - "will they watch this video?"

video views are weighted heavily, especially past a minimum watch time.

making the video engaging & watchable is the key here.

---

TIER 2: THESE ARE "STRONG" SIGNALS

not as heavy as tier 1, but still move the needle significantly.

---

6/ P(reply) - "will they reply to this?"

this can be triggered via. questions, relatable experiences, controversial takes.

---

7/ P(profile_click) - "will they click your profile?"

can be triggered via. authority signals & "who IS this person?" intrigue.

---

8/ P(share) - "will they share this externally?"

triggered by posting useful content people want to send to specific people.

---

9/ P(dwell) - "will they stop scrolling?"

this is a binary version of dwell_time. did they pause at all, yes or no?

triggered by visual hooks. pattern interrupts. anything that breaks the scroll.

---

TIER 3: SUPPORTING METRICS

these help but won't carry a post alone.

---

10/ P(favorite) - "will they like this?"

seems to matter less than we think. likes are viewed as a cheap low effort signal.

---

11/ P(click) - "will they click to expand?"

triggered by truncated content. "see more". images that need expanding.

---

12/ P(photo_expand) - "will they tap to see the full image?"

triggered by detailed infographics. screenshots with small text. images where details matter.

---

13/ P(quoted_click) - "will they click the quoted tweet?"

this shows your quote added enough value that they want the original context.

---

14/ P(share_via_dm) + P(share_via_copy_link)

specific share intents. dm shares are especially valuable it seems.

---

TIER 4: NEGATIVE WEIGHTS

these SUBTRACT from your score & hurt your reach

---

15/ P(not_interested) - "will they hit 'not interested'?"

heavy negative weight, this tanks your score.

this is triggered via. irrelevant content. clickbait that doesn't deliver. content that doesn't match what you usually post.

---

16/ P(mute_author) - "will they mute you?"

triggered posting too much. same topic repeatedly. being annoying.

---

17/ P(block_author) - "will they block you?"

triggered by toxicity.

---

18/ P(report) - "will they report this post?"

triggered by content thats against TOS. spam.

---

MULTIPLIERS

after scoring, the algo applies these:

---

19/ author diversity multiplier

X now penalizes your 2nd, 3rd, 4th posts in someone's feed.

the math:
- 1st post: 100% score
- 2nd post: ~70% score
- 3rd post: ~50% score
- it keeps decaying

this means that posting 10x a day = each post performs worse, best to space them out.

---

20/ in-network boost

posts from accounts you follow get a multiplier > 1.0

posts from strangers get multiplied by a fraction (< 1.0)

this means that 1,000 real followers > 10,000 random impressions. you have to build your follower base.

---

21/ recency filter

what it does: posts older than 7 days get filtered out entirely.

any tweet from a week ago is completely dead. the algo favors fresh content.

---

22/ user engagement history (the personalization layer)

X tracks each user's last 128 engagements.

the algo learns: "this user likes ai threads" or "this user engages with sports"

your post is scored against THEIR history.

this means that your post can be giga-specific to a certain users & it'll only be shown to those specific types of users. it's hyper-personalized.

---

here's the cheatsheet based on all of this...

MAXIMIZE:
- retweetability 
- quote tweetability
- dwell time potential 
- follow incentive (make them want more)
- reply bait

MINIMIZE:
- mutes
- blocks 
- "not interested"
- reports 

NOTES:
- followers = huge advantage
- posting too much = penalty
- fresh content = rewarded

that's basically the whole system, it's kind of similar to what it used to be, except now you have AI rank your posts before they even get any engagement.

here's how to use claude code to generate UI that's ACTUALLY good...

first you'll need to find a UI you want to take inspiration from & clone a "reference" page

this'll be a page you want to take inspiration from so that claude code has a good reference point to start with

claude cannot extract accurate styling from screenshots alone, but it's VERY good at replicating styles when you give it the exact CSS, you need to add that context

whatever reference styling you initially give claude, it'll stick to for whatever new feature or component you add afterwards so it's very important to get a good reference initially

to replicate a page, don't just send a screenshot of a UI that you like...

instead, send a screenshot + the CSS of that website (click inspect, highlight HTML & copy everything in the styles tab

use this prompt "help me rebuild the exact same UI design as the screenshot in a single html file, i've attached the extracted css"

now when claude generates it, it'll look better than if you sent the screenshot without the CSS, but still not perfect...

to perfect it, download an extension called visbug & copy the CSS of each individual element...

then ask claude code to apply it to that specific element on your copied page

once it's a pixel-perfect copy of the reference page, you'll want to extract a "style-guide" markdown file to be able to create new pages in the same exact style

use this prompt in claude code to extract the markdown:

"help me generate a detailed style guide as a markdown file. in the style guide you must include the following things: overview, color palette, typography (pay attention to font weight, font size and how different fonts have been used together in the project), spacing system, component styles, shadows & elevation, animations & transitions, border radius, opacity & transparency, common tailwind css usage in project, example component reference design code and so on... provide detailed analysis and descriptions to the project style system, and don't miss any important details"

now you can download this style guide & put it in any project

once it's in the codebase, when you ask claude to generate a UI in this style for anything whatsoever, it'll generate UI in that exact style
